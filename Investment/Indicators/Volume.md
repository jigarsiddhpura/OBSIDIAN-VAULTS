
### Volume — the core validator

**Why it matters:** Price can move on thin trading — a few large buy orders can push a stock up 1-2% with almost no real conviction behind it. **Volume tells you how many market participants agreed with that move**. A breakout above resistance on low volume is often a trap: early buyers get excited, price pokes above resistance value, but there isn't enough real demand behind it to hold, so it drifts back down once those initial buyers stop.

**How to use it to determine if its a BREAKOUT:**

- Calculate the 20-day average volume (just the mean of the last 20 trading days' volume).
- Compare today's volume (the breakout day) to that average.
- **Rule of thumb:** breakout volume should be at least 1.5x the 20-day average to be taken seriously. 2x+ is strong. If today's volume was _below_ average, treat the closing with suspicion — it may not hold.
- Bonus check: was volume also elevated on the 1-2 days _before_ the breakout (accumulation building into it), or did it spike only today (single-day event, possibly news-driven and less durable)?

### 20-day average volume — why 20 days specifically

20 trading days ≈ one calendar month. It's short enough to reflect _current_ trading interest (not stale data from 3 months ago) but long enough to smooth out random daily noise. This is your baseline — everything else in point 1 is relative to this number. Without it, "high volume" is meaningless; you need the average as the yardstick.

**2.64M is your 20-day average volume**.

Here's how to read it:

- The **white line** running across your volume bars is the Moving Average(20) line you set up.
- The **white box on the right (2.64M)** is where that line currently sits on the value scale — that's the MA(20) value.
- The **teal box (3.67M)** corresponds to the actual volume of the specific bar your cursor/crosshair is on (the tall green bar) — meaning that day's volume was 3.67M shares, which is well above the 20-day average of 2.64M.

So in plain terms: on that particular day, volume (3.67M) was noticeably higher than the 20-day average (2.64M) — roughly 1.4x average volume, which would typically be read as unusually high activity for that stock that day.
