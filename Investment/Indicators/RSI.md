
### RSI (Relative Strength Index) — momentum fuel gauge

**What it measures:** RSI (0-100) compares the size of *recent up-moves to recent down-moves* over a ***lookback period*** (usually 14 days). 

> It's not about price level, it's about _how hard_ the stock has been pushing in one direction.

**Why it matters for breakout:** A breakout needs momentum behind it to continue. But momentum can also be "**spent**" — if RSI is already very high, it means the stock has already made a big, fast move, and there's less fuel left for the next leg.

**How to read it here:**

- **RSI 55-70:** Healthy continuation zone. Momentum is positive but not exhausted
- **RSI 70+:** Overbought. Doesn't mean "must crash," but statistically raises the odds of a pause or pullback _before_ further upside. If RSI is 75-80 today, don't expect a straight run to 52W HIGH expect a cool-off first, possibly back towards a little, before another attempt higher.
- **RSI below 50 despite the price breakout:** A red flag — price and momentum disagreeing (bearish divergence), meaning the breakout is weak underneath the surface.

**Yellow line = RSI-based Moving Average** ([[RSI-MA]])

> [!tip] This is the single most useful number to check first, because it directly answers "is there room left to run, or is this move already tired."

**How to read momentum from it (ideal scenario):**

- *RSI above **70*** → generally considered overbought (momentum may be stretched, potential pullback zone).
- *RSI below **30*** → generally considered oversold (momentum may be exhausted downward, potential bounce zone).
- *RSI hovering around **50*** → neutral, no strong momentum either way.
- Also watch for **divergence** — if price makes a new high but RSI makes a lower high, that's often a signal momentum is fading even as price keeps climbing.
