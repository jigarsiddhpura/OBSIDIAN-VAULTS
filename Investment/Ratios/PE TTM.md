
**P/E (Price-to-Earnings, Trailing Twelve Months)** tells you how many rupees an investor is paying today for every ₹1 of profit the company earned in the _last completed 12 months_.

**Formula:**

```
P/E (TTM) = Current Share Price ÷ TTM Earnings Per Share (EPS)
```

or equivalently:

```
P/E (TTM) = Current Market Cap ÷ TTM Net Profit
```

**Using Anant Raj's own numbers:**

- Current price: ~₹583
- FY26 (Apr'25–Mar'26) EPS was ~₹15.81 per share (this is what "TTM" means here — the most recent 4 quarters, which for ARL right now happens to line up with FY26 since Q4 FY26 just closed)
- P/E = 583 ÷ 15.81 ≈ **36.9x**

**So the market is paying ~37 years' worth of _current_ annual earnings for one share**, assuming profit never changed. That's the "TTM" (backward-looking) version — as opposed to "Forward P/E," which uses _analysts' estimated future_ earnings instead of past earnings (you saw ~54.9x peer average and ~38.8x for ARL on a forward basis in the Simply Wall St data — different because it's dividing today's price by _next year's expected_ profit, not last year's actual profit).

**Why it matters:** A P/E of 37x only makes sense if you believe profit will keep growing fast enough to "earn into" that price over time. That's why I compared it to ARL's own 10-year average P/E (~31.6x) and to peers (~35-55x+) rather than judging it in isolation — a P/E number alone tells you almost nothing; it's only useful _relative to_ growth rate, own history, or peers.
